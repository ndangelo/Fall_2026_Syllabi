Course description

The course introduces social design to develop skills for navigating the complexity of social design projects. This class aims to familiarize students with the evolving role of designers in addressing complex social issues. Social design involves deepening understanding through readings and practical project work. The course will teach students to analyze customs, habits, and cultural differences to create innovative products, services, and social practices.

Prerequisites

AMDE 155 and 255

Student learning outcomes

Students will:

  • Define a community's requirements and aspirations at the core of the design process in order to accurately discern their genuine needs.
  • Explain how they would implement social design in an organizational, professional, or personal setting, detailing their comprehension of the process.
  • Employ a systems approach to thinking that considers political, economic, cultural, and environmental systems.
  • Employ current or emerging social concerns by integrating them into the design of products, services, and experiences.
  • Design for social contexts and outcomes, and gain hands-on experience in addressing social, environmental, or systemic challenges using social design principles.
  • Compare the effectiveness or quality of a social design by generating work that connects well with a specific target audience.

Course content

Students will gain hands-on experience utilizing their design skills toward social design through projects and exercises such as:

  • Establishing social design concepts and techniques.
  • Collecting and implementing data in social design.
  • Using hand-drawn type to illustrate a belief about the future of a specific topic.
  • Collecting data derived from the carbon footprint of a favorite food.
  • Using composite imagery to create a poster that accentuates a social issue or cause.
  • Creating a visual narrative inspired by a universally shared story theme, emphasizing experimental approaches in social design and art.
  • Design and Democracy: a poster highlighting differences in understanding the beliefs held by two political figures.
  • A six-word social design poster, booklet, or Instagram Story.
  • Ten persuasive posters for public display: open concept.

Sensitive topics, constructive dialogue, and hate speech

Since this course is titled “Social Design Studio,” we will be dealing with potentially sensitive topics. These may include:

  • Trauma, abuse, violence, or harassment.
  • Mental health struggles, anxiety, depression, and suicide.
  • Grief, loss, or illness.
  • Eating disorders, body image, body positivity, or weight loss.
  • Diet or eating patterns, such as veganism, keto, or intermittent fasting.
  • Addictions, substance abuse, or recovery.
  • Political, religious, socioeconomic, or ideological commentary.
  • Personal views and beliefs.
  • Personal stories that include profanity or distressing events.
  • Manifestation.
  • Even positivity that people could consider “toxic positivity.”

These topics will be introduced in class, and classmates may include their own perceptions of these and other issues in their projects. Please speak with me if you feel uncomfortable with any project or discussion, and review this information to consider whether the course is a good fit for you. Constructive dialogue about projects and issues is encouraged, as long as we maintain decorum.

Hate speech is prohibited. This includes speech that attacks individuals or groups based on identity; expression intended to demean and that may cause psychological injury, fear, or social harm; and forms ranging from explicit slurs and calls for violence to coded language or propaganda that creates a hostile environment.

Instructional process

  • Lessons: daily and weekly projects as assigned by the instructor.
  • Lectures: 35–45-minute lectures per week.
  • Weekly assignments: software technique assignments as assigned.
  • Critiques: a class critique will follow assignments when appropriate.
  • Lab time: in-class time will be available to work on projects and practice techniques.
Keep your work. Do not throw anything away. There will be individual critiques of your body of work at the end of the semester.

All students must back up their work. Loss of a project through technology failure is not an excuse for a late assignment.

Grading criteria and evaluation

Grades are based on completing projects on time, proper use of techniques demonstrated in previous AMDE courses, proper use of class-specific techniques, and meeting deadlines. If a project is not completed on time by the due date, the syllabus states that the subsequent grade will be a failing one.

Evaluation may consider:

  • Clarity of digital communication.
  • Concept and creativity.
  • Ability to follow instructions and meet deadlines.
  • Computer skills and verbal presentation.
  • Thoughtful, original, creative concepts.
  • Craftsmanship and mastery of application techniques.
  • Initiative and imaginative responses.
  • Class participation and discussion.
  • Individual progress throughout the semester.
  • Attendance.

Attendance policies

  • Regular class attendance is mandatory. Lectures, directions, and critiques will not be repeated. Each student is expected to stay the entire class.
  • One unexcused absence is permitted. More than one absence will lower the final grade. Four absences will be grounds for a failing grade regardless of work completed. Attendance is taken during the first 15 minutes.
  • Three tardies equal one absence. If you know in advance that you will be late or absent, email the instructor ahead of time.
  • A project not ready at the beginning of class or submitted after a critique on the due date will be penalized by one letter grade for each class it is late. Absence or limited lab access is not an excuse for late work. Projects more than three days late will not be accepted. Plan ahead for lab time.
  • Assignments are due at the start of class. Present projects during critiques, participate in discussion, respect classmates and give presenters your undivided attention. Class disturbances will not be tolerated. Turn off cellular phones; texting is not allowed during the course.

NETIQUETTE

Definition: Etiquette used in an online chat or discussion via the internet. “Netiquette” gives us the guidelines to use to have creative, critical and polite conversations in an online environment. The online conversation is read by people on a computer screen. It can be difficult to read emotion through the written word, so using appropriate text and descriptive language is very important. Also when in an online conversation a user must remember to read the message as it stands and not read into the message what may not be there.

The following rules will assist you in adhering to proper netiquette in this course:

Would I say this to someone if we were face to face?

If not, rewrite the post in a way that reflects how you would speak if you were in the same room having the same conversation.

Do you want what you are publishing shared and possibly seen by others outside this conversation?

Follow the assignment/discussion board rules set by the moderator or instructor.

Make an attempt at quality written work.

Check your facts

Cite sources, if using material from others in your discussion

Stay away from hearsay and gossip

Share information, links and sources that may assist others in your conversation

Be careful with negative talk, personal opinion and emotional responses.

Respect the privacy of your classmates, do not share their stories without permission.

PLAGIARISM

Plagiarism is using the language, ideas, and thoughts of another without acknowledgement. It is dishonest. Whenever you use any idea derived substantially and directly from a published work, from a fellow student, or from any other source, you must explicitly acknowledge the nature and extent of your indebtedness. Whenever you borrow three or more consecutive words, an original term, or a distinctive turn of phrase from a published work, from a fellow student, or from any other source, you must enclose the borrowed element in quotation marks and explicitly acknowledge your indebtedness.

Plagiarism of any assignment will result in a failing grade for that work. All cases of plagiarism will be sent to the Student Faculty Judicial Board.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICY (INCLUDING AI USAGE)

All students are expected to engage in academically honest work. In an academic environment, everything we do is based on the integrity of the ideas we exchange. Therefore, academic integrity is the foundation of academic work and it's vital that all students uphold this principle-- whether using AI-based tools such as ChatGPT or otherwise. Academic honesty means precisely and accurately identifying facts and accurately reporting the sources of ideas we use in our work. Academic dishonesty is representing someone else’s work as your own and can sometimes occur unintentionally when a student fails to understand the proper methods of documentation. You may not submit any work generated by an AI program as your own. If you include material generated by an AI program, it must be cited like any other reference material (with due consideration for the quality of the reference, which may be poor).

Let me know if you have any questions about how to document outside sources or visit the Writing Studio in Kemp Library.

Willful academic dishonesty occurs when a student purposefully tries to represent another’s ideas as his/her own in order to avoid doing assigned work, for example cutting and pasting material from an internet site, copying text from a print source, purchasing or stealing a paper, presentation or other work written by someone else, cheating on exams or quizzes, etc. You will find a complete list in the Student Handbook. These standards apply to all work done for this class not just major projects. All cases of suspected academic dishonesty will be referred to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards. I reserve the right to fail any student found responsible for violations of academic integrity.

TITLE IX AND ESU

East Stroudsburg University and its faculty are committed to assuring a safe and productive educational environment for all students. In order to meet this commitment and to comply with Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 and guidance from the Office for Civil Rights, Title IX requires University faculty members to report incidents of sexual discrimination, including sexual violence, shared by students to the University’s Title IX Coordinator. Accordingly, if a student shares information about any incidents of sexual discrimination or sexual violence during a classroom discussion, in a writing assignment for a class, or in other contexts, faculty must report that information to the Title IX Coordinator. This information will only be shared with the Title IX Coordinator, who is the individual on campus designated to respond to reports of discrimination or sexual violence. While the Title IX Coordinator is not a confidential source of support, they will address matters reported to them with sensitivity and will keep your information as private as possible.

Additionally, faculty members are obligated to report sexual violence or any other abuse of a student who was, or is, a child (a person under 18 years of age) when the abuse allegedly occurred, to the person designated in the University’s Protection of Minors Policy.

Information regarding the reporting of sexual violence and the resources that are available to victims of sexual violence is set forth at: https://www.esu.edu/title-ix/
