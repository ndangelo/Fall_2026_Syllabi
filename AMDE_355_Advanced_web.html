Course description

This course extends the concepts covered in introductory web design. Students solve advanced communication and design problems while addressing technical considerations such as responsive production, accessibility, usability, web standards, security, and user experience. Through hands-on studio projects, students develop interactive, multimedia-rich websites and practice contemporary design, development, testing, and deployment workflows.

Course goal
Develop advanced web design and production skills through experimentation, iterative development, critique, and the creation of functional, accessible, and responsive web experiences.

Student learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Apply advanced HTML and CSS techniques to create responsive, standards-compliant web interfaces.
  2. Design effective web interfaces using visual communication, typography, information architecture, usability, and accessibility principles.
  3. Develop interactive web experiences using JavaScript and contemporary web technologies.
  4. Integrate optimized images, audio, video, animation, and other multimedia into functional web projects.
  5. Evaluate websites for usability, accessibility, performance, and security, and implement appropriate improvements.
  6. Create and deploy a functional website demonstrating advanced web design and development skills.
  7. Critique and refine web design projects in response to design goals, technical requirements, and feedback.

Instructional process

AMDE 355 is organized as an advanced, project-based studio. Class meetings combine technical demonstrations and instruction with sustained hands-on development, troubleshooting, individual feedback, and critique.

  • Instructor demonstrations and technical mini-lessons
  • Studio/lab time for project development and experimentation
  • Weekly technical exercises and applied design assignments
  • Project critiques, presentations, and peer feedback
  • Iterative testing, revision, and final website production

Students are responsible for backing up their work regularly and maintaining project files throughout the semester.

Project rubric

Projects are evaluated according to the following criteria:

  • Design and communication: clear purpose, audience awareness, visual hierarchy, and effective interface decisions.
  • Technical execution: appropriate use of HTML, CSS, JavaScript, responsive techniques, and relevant tools.
  • Usability and accessibility: clear navigation, readable content, inclusive design, and testing across screen sizes.
  • Creative development: experimentation, originality, problem-solving, and thoughtful iteration.
  • Craftsmanship and completion: working functionality, clean presentation, testing, refinement, and meeting project requirements.
  • Critique and reflection: ability to explain design decisions, respond to feedback, and identify improvements.

Evaluation and studio expectations

Students are expected to participate in studio activities, arrive prepared to work, meet project deadlines, present work for critique, and provide constructive feedback. Project critiques may include discussion of design decisions, code structure, functionality, accessibility, and the development process.

Academic integrity is reinforced through demonstrations and critiques in which students explain their choices, show how their websites work, and discuss their process.

Attendance policies

One unexcused absence is permitted. More than one absence lowers the final grade. Four absences may result in a failing grade regardless of work completed. Attendance is taken at the end of class. Three tardies equal one absence. If you will be late or absent and know ahead of time, email the instructor in advance.

A project not ready at the beginning of class or handed in after a critique on the due date will be penalized by one letter grade for each class it is late. An absence or limited lab access is not an excuse for late work. Projects more than three days late will not be accepted. All assignments are due at the start of class. Participate actively in critiques, respect classmates, and give presenters your attention. Class disturbances will not be tolerated. Phones should be turned off; texting is not allowed during the course.

Academic integrity policy

All students are expected to engage in academically honest work. Identify facts and sources accurately. Do not submit work generated by an AI program as your own; material generated by AI must be cited like any other reference material. Willful academic dishonesty, including copying from websites, AI tools, print sources, or other people, may be referred to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards and may result in failure.
