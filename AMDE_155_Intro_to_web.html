Course description

Introduction to Web Design introduces students to principles and methods used in web design and production. The skills learned in this course will be used to create simple web applications using web-authoring tools. Assignments provide hands-on experience in introductory web design, including document structure and content, site maintenance, and the use of digital images and graphics.

Course goal
Gain beginning skills in web development, including an understanding of web design, usability, UXD, information architecture (IA), and web authoring.

Student learning objectives

Students will:

  • Demonstrate an understanding of basic web design, usability, and web authoring tools.
  • Evaluate websites for information organization, content representation, and site navigation.
  • Evaluate and compare web development software and tools.
  • Design and build aesthetically pleasing static websites using WYSIWYG (“what you see is what you get”) editors and design tools.
  • Design web media effectively for identified target markets.
  • Practice legal use of images and content.

Course content

To become proficient in web design and programming/markup languages, students will work with:

  1. HTML markup
  2. Website production
  3. Establishing a base for a webpage
  4. Basic CSS and HTML5 styles
  5. Content Management Systems (CMS)
  6. CMS and WordPress
  7. Simple WordPress blog creation
  8. Jetpack and Google Analytics
  9. Business website production

Instructional process

  1. Lessons: daily and weekly projects as assigned by the professor.
  2. Lectures: 30–40-minute lectures per week.
  3. Weekly assignments: software technique assignments as assigned.
  4. Critiques: class critiques will follow assignments when appropriate.
  5. Lab time: in-class time to work on projects and practice techniques.

This class requires homework, which may be up to 10 hours weekly, depending on time spent in class for projects. Please schedule your time accordingly. Responsibilities at work or home will not be accepted as excuses for late or missing projects.

Attendance policies

One unexcused absence is permitted. More than one absence lowers the final grade. Four absences may result in a failing grade regardless of work completed. Attendance is taken at the end of class. Three tardies equal one absence. If you will be late or absent and know ahead of time, email the instructor in advance.

A project not ready at the beginning of class or handed in after a critique on the due date will be penalized by one letter grade for each class it is late. An absence or limited lab access is not an excuse for late work. Projects more than three days late will not be accepted. All assignments are due at the start of class. Participate actively in critiques, respect classmates, and give presenters your attention. Class disturbances will not be tolerated. Phones should be turned off; texting is not allowed during the course.

Academic integrity policy

All students are expected to engage in academically honest work. Identify facts and sources accurately. Do not submit work generated by an AI program as your own; material generated by AI must be cited like any other reference material. Willful academic dishonesty, including copying from websites, AI tools, print sources, or other people, may be referred to the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards and may result in failure.
